01

The card - the on-the-block player as a hero object

The single most important pixels on the screen: the player on the block. Today it is a text row with a colored two-letter chip and no imagery (draft-board-table.tsx:18-32, zero <Image> in the app). EA FC treats one player as a confident, layered card object.

Steal

One player = one layered card: photo integral, rating and position anchored, key numbers legible at a glance. Rarity/tier read from the card's own material, not a label.

Current build falls short

FFDA has no player imagery at all and renders the on-block player as text + a 2-letter chip. No hero object, no material, no depth.

02

The reveal - the on-the-clock moment that has to land

When a player goes on the block, that beat should feel like something. Today it is a spring + a pulse dot with a literal gold hex bypassing the token system (draft/live/client.tsx:985-1097). EA FC's walkout is the reference for a reveal that earns attention.

Walkout tunnel reveal

Dark silhouette to lit reveal, drama scaled to value

Steal

Silhouette then light: the moment escalates, and the escalation is bigger when the player is bigger. A reveal that means something. Restrained to the moment, not every pick.

Current build falls short

FFDA's on-the-clock banner animates (good) but has no imagery and a hardcoded gold that lies about the token system. The beat is there; the payoff is not.

03

The live information hierarchy - advice, budget, feed

Under the card sits the actual work: your max bid, value, estimated cost, budget health, and the live feed. This must be dense and calm at arm's length. EA FC's squad panels handle dense roster state; dense-calm is the reference for density without noise.

Steal

A full squad readable in one look: filled vs open slots, chemistry/health signalled by color, compact per-slot cards. Directly maps to your budget + roster + all-teams read.

Current build falls short
